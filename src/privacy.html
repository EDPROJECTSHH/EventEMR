Mini Emergency & Critical Care Event EMR

Privacy Notice

Last updated 9 October 2026

This application is a medical record used by clinical staff during event medical standby — the temporary treatment posts, observation areas and ambulances set up at sports events, concerts and similar gatherings. It records the care given to people treated at those posts so that the treating team, the receiving hospital and the event organiser have an accurate account of what happened.

This notice describes what the software itself records and where that information goes. It is a description of the application, not a substitute for the privacy policy of the healthcare organisation operating it.

Who is responsible for the records. The healthcare organisation that deploys this application for an event is the controller of the patient information in it, and its own privacy policy, retention schedule and patient-rights procedures govern that information. Questions about a specific record should go to that organisation, through the event medical lead.

What the application records

  • Patient details entered by staff at a post: name, age, sex, an identifying number such as a ticket or participant ID, and arrival time.
  • Clinical information: triage category, observations and vital signs, assessment and progress notes, medicines given, procedures performed, and the outcome — discharged, kept for observation, or transferred to a hospital.
  • Staff details: the names and roles of the team signed in at each post, so that entries can be attributed.
  • Operational messages sent between posts and ambulances through the application's built-in chat.
  • A device identifier generated by the application, which distinguishes one tablet or phone from another so that edits made offline can be merged correctly. It is not an advertising identifier and is not linked to any profile.

Where that information is held

  • On the device. The application is built to work without a network, so records are stored in the browser's own storage on the device that entered them.
  • On the event server. Records are synchronised between the posts of the same event through the application's hosting provider, so that each post can see the whole event. This is what allows a receiving post to see a patient transferred to it.
  • In the event's document folder. When an event is concluded, the application generates the event's record files — patient records as PDF and an event summary as a spreadsheet — and uploads them to the Google Drive folder designated by the operating organisation.

Google Drive access

The application asks for access to Google Drive for one purpose: to place the event's own record files into the designated folder, and to read back the files it has placed there. It does not read, collect or transmit any other content of the authorising account's Drive.

This application's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Access can be withdrawn at any time from the Google account's third-party access settings.

What the application does not do

  • It carries no advertising and no advertising or analytics trackers.
  • It does not sell, rent or share patient information with anyone, and it sends nothing to any third party beyond the hosting and Google Drive storage described above.
  • It does not use patient information to train models or build profiles.
  • It does not request a patient's location, camera or microphone.

How long information is kept

An event's working copy is held only while the event is running. When the event is concluded, its record files are sent to the designated folder, the event is locked, and after a short reopening window the working copy is closed and cleared from the devices that held it. The exported record files then remain in the operating organisation's custody and are retained under that organisation's medical-records policy.

Access, correction and complaints

Requests to see, correct or erase a record, and complaints about how a record was handled, are handled by the operating organisation as the controller of that record. Contact the event medical lead or the organisation's medical records department. Where local law gives a patient rights over their health information, those rights are exercised against that organisation, not against this software.

Changes to this notice

Material changes to what the application records or where it sends records will be reflected here, with the date above updated.
